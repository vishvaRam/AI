What is Backpropagation inside an LLM?

When an LLM generates a token incorrectly during training, it calculates its total error margin (**Loss**). **Backpropagation** is the reverse-engineering stage where this error signal is funneled backward through every matrix layer. By applying the **Calculus Chain Rule**, the model calculates precisely how much each individual parameter contributed to the mistake, converting errors into actionable **Gradients**.

1. The Chain Rule Matrix

An LLM is a complex composite function: $\mathcal{L}(f(g(x)))$. To find the error contribution of an early attention weight, backpropagation multiplies the derivatives of all layers that come after it: $\frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial x} = \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial f} \cdot \frac{\partial f}{\partial g} \cdot \frac{\partial g}{\partial x}$.

2. FFN Error Splitting

Inside the Feed-Forward Network (FFN) blocks, gradients must pass backward through non-linear activation functions like SwiGLU or GELU. The slope of the activation curves scales down noise while preserving vital linguistic correction paths.

3. Attention Gradients

The trickiest part of LLM backpropagation is moving backward through the Self-Attention step ($Q \cdot K^T$). The error gradient splits three ways, tracking how changing the Queries, Keys, or Values would alter the model's contextual understanding.

The Asymmetric Memory Balancing Trick

Backpropagation is the reason training an LLM requires massive amounts of VRAM. To compute derivatives, the system must retain every single activation tensor from the forward pass until the backward pass reaches that specific layer.

⚠️ The VRAM Overhead Problem For a 70-billion parameter model, storing all intermediate activations across billions of tokens quickly triggers out-of-memory errors on standard hardware arrays.
✅ Activation Checkpointing (The Solution) Modern architectures use **Activation Checkpointing**. They discard intermediate tensors from early layers during the forward pass. If the backward pass needs them later, it reruns a mini-forward pass for just that layer on the fly, swapping a little extra compute to save up to 50% of VRAM.
